Distant Reading Analysis Summary

This report synthesizes a computational analysis across Grimms' Fairy Tales, Andersen's Fairy Tales, and The Arabian Nights.

1. Bag of Words (Core Vocabulary)

The shared vocabulary across these three sources is categorized into pillars of governance, family, and enchantment. Significant content words include:

  • Royal Figures: King, Sultan, Emperor, Prince, Princess.
  • Family Units: Son, Daughter, Brother, Sister, Mother.
  • Natural World: Forest, Sea, Garden, Bird, Tree, Water.
  • Magic: Enchanted, Magic, Fairy, Genie, Spell.

3. Topic Modeling

Four primary thematic clusters appear consistently across the collections:

  • Sovereign Trials: Narrative arcs where a monarch's authority initiates a moral test or quest.
  • Metamorphosis: Transformations between human, animal, and mineral states.
  • Familial Betrayal and Loyalty: Conflicts involving sibling rivalry or parental protection.
  • Geography of the Unknown: High frequency of travel across seas and into vast deserts or mountains.

4. Concordance of Key Terms

  • KING / SULTAN: "A certain king had a beautiful garden..." / "The Sultan of the Indies, whose palace is covered with one hundred thousand rubies...".
  • GOLDEN: "...a bird came flying that was of pure gold..." / "The Golden Water... rises up in the middle like a fountain...".
  • BIRD: "Talking Bird, so singular a creature..." / "...a beautiful snow-white bird sitting on a bough...".
  • OLD: "...there lived an old man, who wore plush breeches..." / "He then perceived on the roadside a hideous old man... this old man was a dervish...".
